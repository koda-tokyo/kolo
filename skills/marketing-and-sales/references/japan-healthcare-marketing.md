# Japanese Healthcare Marketing — Compliance & Culture

## The Non-Negotiables

### 1. 薬機法 (PMD Act) — Medical Device Marketing

The Pharmaceutical and Medical Device Act governs what can be said about medical products.

**Prohibited claims (without SaMD certification):**
- ❌ "診断します" (diagnoses)
- ❌ "治療します" (treats)
- ❌ "予防します" (prevents)
- ❌ Any claim of medical efficacy for an uncertified product

**Allowed language for pre-certification products:**
- ✅ "支援します" (supports/assists) — always frame as decision support
- ✅ "効率化します" (improves efficiency) — operational benefit, not clinical
- ✅ "負担を軽減します" (reduces burden) — workflow, not medical outcome
- ✅ "可視化します" (visualizes) — presents information, doesn't interpret

**Rule:** If a claim requires clinical evidence, and you don't have PMDA certification for that claim, don't make it. Frame everything as support/augmentation/efficiency until certified.

### 2. 医療広告ガイドライン (Medical Advertising Guidelines)

MHLW enforces strict rules on medical advertising:

- No before/after comparisons suggesting guaranteed improvement
- No testimonials that imply medical efficacy
- No superlatives suggesting superiority ("最高" "No.1" "唯一")
- No fear-based advertising ("放っておくと危険" / dangerous if left untreated)
- Patient privacy: never use real patient data in marketing without explicit consent

**Comparisons are allowed** but must be:
- Factual and verifiable
- Not denigrating competitors by name
- Based on objective criteria (time saved, not "better AI")

### 3. Data Residency & Security

Japanese hospitals require:
- On-premises deployment capability (オンプレミス)
- Data never leaving Japan (国内データ保存)
- Clear data handling documentation (データ取扱説明書)
- MHLW 3省2ガイドライン compliance for medical information systems

**Always state these upfront.** For Japanese healthcare buyers, this is not a feature — it's table stakes. If you don't say it, they assume you can't do it.

## 敬語 (Keigo) Levels by Audience

### 尊敬語 (Sonkeigo) — Honorific
- **When:** Direct communication with doctors, hospital directors
- **Tone:** Elevates the recipient
- **Example:** いらっしゃいます, おっしゃいます, ご覧になります
- **Marketing use:** Rare in written materials. Too deferential for product pages.

### 謙譲語 (Kenjōgo) — Humble
- **When:** Describing your company's actions to clients
- **Tone:** Lowers yourself
- **Example:** 申し上げます, ご説明いたします, 参ります
- **Marketing use:** In formal proposals, inquiry responses, direct mail to hospitals

### 丁寧語 (Teineigo) — Polite
- **When:** General marketing, product pages, web copy
- **Tone:** Neutral-polite, professional
- **Example:** です・ます調
- **Marketing use:** DEFAULT for all Japanese marketing materials. Safe for all audiences.

### タメ語 (Tamego) — Casual
- **When:** Social media, blog posts targeting younger clinicians
- **Tone:** Approachable, modern
- **Marketing use:** Limited. OK for tech-focused blog posts. Never for official product pages.

**Default rule:** です・ます調 for everything. Deviate only with clear intent.

## Visual & Design Norms for Japanese Medical Marketing

### Color Psychology
- **Blue (青):** Trust, hygiene, technology — dominant in Japanese medical design
- **Green (緑):** Healing, safety, nature — good accent for patient-facing materials
- **White (白):** Cleanliness, professionalism — abundant white space expected
- **Red (赤):** Emergency, urgency — use sparingly, never for positive framing
- **Avoid:** Neon colors, aggressive gradients, dark mode for medical products

### Typography
- Japanese fonts: Noto Sans JP, Hiragino Kaku Gothic, Yu Gothic
- Minimum 14px for body text in Japanese (kanji needs more pixels than Latin)
- Generous line height (1.8 minimum for Japanese text)
- Avoid all-caps equivalents (全角) for body text — hard to read

### Trust Signals (Must Be Visible)
- 医療機器認証 (medical device certification) if applicable
- ISO 13485 / ISO 27001 badges
- 厚生労働省ガイドライン準拠 (MHLW guideline compliance)
- 国内データ保存 (domestic data storage)
- 導入実績 (deployment track record: "X hospitals")

## Communication Patterns That Work in Japanese Healthcare

### Nemawashi (根回し) — Pre-consensus Building
Japanese hospital purchasing decisions are never individual. They flow through:
1. Department chief interest
2. Informal consensus among affected staff
3. Formal evaluation committee
4. Administrative approval

Marketing materials should support each stage:
- Stage 1-2: Technical detail, clinical evidence, peer adoption
- Stage 3-4: Implementation plan, cost analysis, compliance documentation

### Ringi-sho (稟議書) — Formal Approval Document
Hospital purchases require a formal proposal document. Your materials should make this easy:
- Provide implementation cost breakdown in yen
- Include comparison with current process (time/cost)
- Supply compliance documentation in Japanese
- Give deployment timeline with milestones

### Risk Aversion
Japanese medical institutions are extremely risk-averse. Address this explicitly:
- "段階的導入が可能" (phased rollout possible)
- "既存システムと並行運用可能" (parallel operation with existing systems)
- "導入後サポート体制" (post-deployment support structure)
- Reference existing Japanese deployments as proof of stability

## Words to Use / Words to Avoid

### ✅ Use Freely
- 支援 (support), 効率化 (efficiency), 負担軽減 (burden reduction)
- 安心 (peace of mind), 信頼 (trust), 実績 (track record)
- 国内 (domestic/Japan), 安全 (safety), 簡単 (simple)
- 医療従事者 (healthcare professional), 働き方改革 (work-style reform — government initiative)

### ⚠️ Use With Care
- AI (define clearly — Japanese public is skeptical of undefined "AI")
- 自動 (automatic — implies no human oversight, which scares clinicians)
- 革命 (revolution — too aggressive for medical context)
- 最新 (latest — implies unproven)

### ❌ Avoid
- 診断 (diagnosis — reserved for certified medical devices)
- 治療 (treatment — same)
- No.1 / 最高 / 唯一 (superlatives — prohibited in medical advertising)
- 簡単に治る (easily cured — prohibited efficacy claim)
- 絶対 (absolute — never in medical context)
