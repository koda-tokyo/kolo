# Audience Personas — KodaSōken Medical AI

## 1. Doctor / Clinician

### Profile
- **Role:** 医師 (physician), 看護師 (nurse), 臨床検査技師 (clinical technologist)
- **Settings:** 大学病院 (university hospital), 一般病院 (general hospital), クリニック (clinic)
- **Age range:** 30-65
- **Tech comfort:** Medium-low. Uses EMR daily but doesn't care how it works. Wants tools that disappear.

### Daily Reality
- 10-15 minute consultations per patient
- 2-4 hours of documentation after clinical hours
- Constant context-switching between EMR, lab results, imaging, referrals
- Chronic time poverty — every extra click is a tax

### What They Care About
1. Patient outcomes (primary)
2. Workflow disruption (dealbreaker)
3. Clinical evidence and peer adoption
4. Regulatory compliance (implicit trust in hospital approval)
5. Learning curve (want near-zero)

### What They Don't Care About
- AI architecture
- Venture backing
- Market disruption narratives
- Technical specs

### Language That Works
- "During a typical consultation..." (situational, relatable)
- "Your EMR, enhanced" (integration, not replacement)
- "Adopted by X hospitals in Japan" (peer proof)
- "Documentation time reduced by X%" (quantified relief)
- Lead with the clinical scenario, not the product name

### Language That Fails
- "Revolutionary AI-powered solution"
- "Next-generation platform"
- "Disrupting healthcare"
- Any sentence where the product is the hero instead of the doctor

### Japanese Notes
- です・ます調 is standard
- 先生 (sensei) for direct communication (marketing materials: 医師)
- Cite 学会 (academic society) evidence where available
- 薬機法: never claim "diagnosis" or "treatment" unless certified as SaMD

## 2. Hospital Administrator

### Profile
- **Role:** 病院長 (hospital director), 事務長 (administrative director), 経営企画 (corporate planning)
- **Settings:** 中小病院 (SME hospitals, 50-300 beds), 大規模病院 (large hospitals, 300+ beds)
- **Age range:** 45-65
- **Tech comfort:** Medium. Evaluates systems, doesn't use them daily.

### Daily Reality
- Staffing shortages (2024 医師働き方改革 compliance pressure)
- 診療報酬改定 (biennial reimbursement revision) impacts
- MHLW audits and documentation requirements
- Balancing clinical quality with financial sustainability
- Evaluating multiple vendor claims simultaneously

### What They Care About
1. Cost reduction and ROI timeline
2. Staff workload reduction (turnover prevention)
3. Compliance coverage (MHLW, 診療報酬, ISO)
4. Implementation feasibility (timeline, training, disruption)
5. Risk mitigation (data residency, security, vendor stability)

### What They Don't Care About
- AI model names
- GitHub stars
- Tech stack details beyond "Linux-based, on-prem capable"

### Language That Works
- "Reduce overtime documentation by X hours/month"
- "MHLW guideline compliant, on-premises deployment"
- "Staff training: 2 hours. Full rollout: 4 weeks."
- "ROI within 6 months of deployment"
- Concrete yen figures and percentages

### Language That Fails
- "Cutting-edge deep learning"
- "Move fast and break things" (horrifying in healthcare context)
- Vague promises without numbers

### Japanese Notes
- 丁寧語 (polite, formal) — not casual
- Reference 厚生労働省 (MHLW) by name
- Mention 診療報酬改定 relevance explicitly
- Data residency in Japan is non-negotiable — say it upfront
- 導入実績 (deployment track record) matters more than features

## 3. Investor / Partner

### Profile
- **Role:** VC partner, corporate VC, strategic investor, government fund (NEDO/METI)
- **Geography:** Japan domestic + global (Singapore, UAE, US)
- **Age range:** 35-60
- **Tech comfort:** High. Evaluates defensibility, not features.

### Daily Reality
- Seeing 20+ pitch decks per week
- Pattern-matching against previous healthcare AI failures
- Looking for regulatory moat as much as technical moat
- Often non-clinical — needs the healthcare context explained

### What They Care About
1. Market size and structure (TAM in beds/yen, not just "aging population")
2. Competitive moat (regulatory, data network, technical, distribution)
3. Unit economics (per-hospital CAC, LTV, gross margin)
4. Team (healthcare domain credibility + technical execution)
5. Exit path or sustainable growth trajectory

### What They Don't Care About
- Detailed feature lists
- UI screenshots (unless proving adoption simplicity)
- Vague "AI for healthcare" positioning

### Language That Works
- "X million hospital beds in Japan, Y% addressable today"
- "Regulatory barrier: PMD Act certification required — we are X months into process"
- "Per-hospital economics: ¥X CAC, ¥Y annual revenue, Z-month payback"
- "Competition cannot replicate the MHLW compliance layer without Y years"
- Clear, defensible category definition

### Language That Fails
- "We're the Uber of healthcare"
- "Massive TAM: global healthcare is $X trillion"
- "Proprietary AI" without explaining the actual moat
- Buzzword-heavy decks

### Japanese Notes
- です・ます調 acceptable for Japanese investors
- For global investors: English is standard, Japan-specific metrics need explaining
- Japanese VCs: emphasize regulatory moat and domestic adoption
- Global VCs: emphasize scalability beyond Japan
- 経産省 (METI) / NEDO grants as validation signal, not revenue

## 4. General Public / Patient

### Profile
- **Role:** Patient, family member, potential user, general visitor
- **Age range:** 20-80
- **Tech comfort:** Variable. Assume low.
- **Context:** May be anxious, searching while dealing with health concerns

### Daily Reality
- Overwhelmed by medical information online
- Distrust of "AI" in healthcare without clear explanation
- Wants to know: "Is this for me? Is it safe? Do doctors trust it?"
- May be reading in waiting room, on mobile, distracted

### What They Care About
1. "What does this mean for me or my family?"
2. Safety and trust
3. Simplicity of explanation
4. Doctor endorsement (social proof)

### What They Don't Care About
- Technical architecture
- Corporate background
- Market positioning
- Any jargon whatsoever

### Language That Works
- "Less waiting, more time with your doctor"
- "Used in X hospitals across Japan"
- "Your doctor sees the full picture, faster"
- "Built to meet Japan's strictest medical safety standards"
- Warm, concrete, benefit-led, 3rd-grade reading level

### Language That Fails
- "AI-powered clinical decision support"
- "Leveraging deep learning to optimize"
- "Enterprise-grade platform"
- Anything that sounds like it was written for a white paper

### Japanese Notes
- です・ます調, warm tone
- Avoid カタカナ overload (AI is OK; プラットフォームソリューション is not)
- Use 安心 (peace of mind) and 信頼 (trust) explicitly
- Social proof: "X病院で導入" (deployed at X hospitals)
- Mobile-first: most visitors will be on smartphones
