# Compliance Operations — KodaSōken

## Philosophy

Compliance at KodaSōken means: we file the right things at the right time, we keep documents we're legally required to keep, and we don't surprise the tax office or the labor bureau. It's not a department — it's a calendar, a checklist, and a shared responsibility.

**Golden rule:** A missed filing deadline is always worse than an imperfect filing. File on time, amend later if needed.

## Compliance Calendar

Maintain a single shared compliance calendar (Google Calendar or similar). Every deadline gets an event with:
- One-week warning notification
- One-day warning notification
- Owner name (person responsible, not "the company")
- Link to relevant template or past filing

### Quarterly Health Checks for Compliance
- Is the company's registered address (tōki) current at the Legal Affairs Bureau?
- Are all board members' terms current?
- Is the corporate seal (jitsuin) secure and its use logged?
- Are articles of incorporation (teikan) accessible and current?
- Any regulatory changes affecting operations? (Check with zeirishi or industry association)

## Document Retention

### Retention periods (Japan statutory minimums):

| Document Type | Retention Period | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Financial books, ledgers, journals | 10 years (KK) | Companies Act |
| Financial statements and supporting docs | 10 years | Companies Act |
| Tax-related documents | 7 years (corporate) | Corporation Tax Act |
| Employee records (payroll, attendance) | 5 years | Labor Standards Act |
| Contracts (general) | 5 years after expiry | Civil Code (recommended) |
| Board meeting minutes | 10 years (permanent preferred) | Companies Act |
| Shareholder registry | Permanent | Companies Act |
| Invoices (for consumption tax) | 7 years | Consumption Tax Act |
| Medical examination records | 5 years | Industrial Safety and Health Act |

### Digital vs. Physical:
- Electronic records are legally valid in Japan for most documents (e-Document Act).
- Timestamps are required for certain electronic tax documents (Electronic Bookkeeping Act — denshi chōbo hozon-hō).
- If using a scanner to digitize: meet JIIMA certification standards for the scanner/software.
- **Practical default:** Keep originals for contracts, board minutes, and any documents with seals (inkan). Digitize everything else.

### Destruction protocol:
- When retention period expires, destroy securely (shred physical, permanent-delete digital).
- Log destruction: date | document type | retention period end | destroyed by.
- Never destroy documents subject to litigation hold, ongoing audit, or investigation.

## Audit Preparation

At KodaSōken's scale, external audits are typically:
- **Tax audit (zeimu chōsa):** By the National Tax Agency. Can happen anytime, usually every 3-5 years. Focus: proper revenue recognition, expense substantiation, withholding tax compliance.
- **Labor audit (rōdō kijun kantokusho chōsa):** By the Labor Standards Inspection Office. Focus: working hours, overtime pay, workplace safety.
- **Social insurance audit:** By the Japan Pension Service. Focus: proper enrollment and premium calculation.

### Pre-audit checklist (maintain as a living document):
1. All tax returns filed on time for past 3 years
2. Financial records complete and match tax returns
3. Employee work hour records maintained (even for exempt employees, track hours — not for pay, but for labor audit)
4. Contractor agreements clearly distinguish from employment
5. Receipts and expense substantiation organized by month
6. Corporate seal usage log up to date
7. Board meeting minutes signed and filed

### During an audit:
- **Single point of contact:** Designate one person (operations lead or finance lead) to interface with auditors.
- **Scope control:** Answer what's asked. Don't volunteer unrelated documents.
- **Professional support:** Always have your zeirishi present (or available by phone) during a tax audit. During a labor audit, have your shakai rōmu shoshi available.
- **Response time:** Meet deadlines. If more time is needed, request a formal extension in writing.

### After an audit:
- Address findings within the timeline specified.
- Update processes to prevent recurrence. (See process-design.md.)
- File corrected returns if required.

## Regulatory Reporting

### Ongoing obligations for a Japanese KK:
- Annual financial statement publication (kessan kōkoku) — can be on company website
- Updates to corporate registry (tōki) for: director changes, address changes, capital changes, purpose changes
- Significant fact reporting if applicable (e.g., loss of major client if material to shareholders)
- Any industry-specific licenses or permits for KodaSōken's services (confirm with counsel if applicable)

### Changes requiring corporate registry update (file within 2 weeks):
- Director/officer appointment, resignation, or term expiry
- Change of registered head office address
- Amendment to articles of incorporation
- Capital increase or decrease
- Merger, split, or dissolution

## Insurance Requirements

For a small consulting firm in Japan, minimum insurance coverage:

| Insurance Type | Purpose | Recommended Level |
|---|---|---|
| Professional indemnity / E&O | Client claims for errors in advice/deliverables | ¥100M-¥300M aggregate (check client contract requirements) |
| Workers' accident compensation (rōsai hoken) | Mandatory for employees | Statutory (government insurance) |
| General liability | Third-party injury/property at office | ¥100M |
| Cyber liability | Data breach, system downtime | ¥50M-¥100M (increasingly required by clients) |
| Directors & officers (D&O) | Personal liability for management decisions | ¥100M (required if investors are involved) |
| Employment practices liability | Wrongful termination, harassment claims | ¥50M (consider when >10 employees) |

Review coverage annually before renewal. Client contracts may specify higher limits — those override these defaults.

## External Resources

- **Labor and social insurance attorney (shakai rōmu shoshi):** Primary advisor for labor compliance, social insurance, and labor audits.
