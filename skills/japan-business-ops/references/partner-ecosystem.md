## 1. Partner Models Overview

Japanese B2B markets predominantly run through intermediaries. Direct sales models rarely succeed without adaptation.

| Model | Japanese | Function | Margin Expectation | Control Level |
|-------|----------|----------|-------------------|---------------|
| Distributor | 代理店 (Dairiten) | Resells; handles local sales/support | 20-40% | Low |
| System Integrator | SIer (エスアイヤー) | Integrates into larger system; prime contractor | 25-50% | Low-Medium |
| OEM | OEM (OEM提供) | White-label; sells as their own product | Negotiated | Very Low |
| Sales Agent | 販売代理店 | Introduces deals; doesn't transact | 10-20% commission | Medium |
| Joint Venture | 合弁会社 (Gōben Gaisha) | Shared entity; shared risk/reward | Equity split | Shared |

## 2. 代理店 (Distributor/Agent) Model

### Structure
- Distributor purchases product from vendor at wholesale price
- Distributor sets end-user price (or follows vendor MSRP)
- Distributor handles: sales, first-line support, Japanese documentation
- Vendor provides: training, second-line support, product updates

### Types of Distributors
| Type | Profile | Best For |
|------|---------|----------|
| Major Trading Company | 総合商社 (Sōgō Shōsha) — Mitsubishi, Mitsui, Itochu | Broad market access; credibility signal |
| Specialized Distributor | 専門商社 — Vertical-focused | Domain expertise; existing customer base |
| Regional Distributor | 地域代理店 — Single prefecture/region | Local government; SME market |
| Online Reseller | 販売店 — E-commerce focused | Low-touch products; volume |

### Distributor Economics
- Standard margin: 25-40% for enterprise software
- Hardware: 15-25% margin
- SaaS: 20-30% margin (recurring)
- Higher margins expected for Japanese-language support burden
- Price protection (値崩れ防止) expectations if vendor sells direct

### Key Contract Provisions
- Territory definition (販売地域制限)
- Exclusivity terms (独占販売権) and minimum performance thresholds
- Non-compete during and post-agreement
- IP ownership and brand usage
- Termination notice period: typically 6-12 months (longer than Western norms)
- 協議条項 (Consultation clause): dispute resolution via negotiation before litigation

## 3. SIer (System Integrator) Partnerships

### The SIer Landscape
System integrators dominate Japanese enterprise IT procurement. Major players:

| Tier | Examples | Revenue Range |
|------|----------|---------------|
| Mega-SI | NTT Data, Fujitsu, NEC, Hitachi | ¥1T+ |
| Mid-SI | SCSK, CTC, NS Solutions | ¥200B-1T |
| Niche SI | Vertical/technology specialists | ¥10-200B |
| Independent | Small consultancies | <¥10B |

### How SIers Work with Foreign Vendors
- SIer is the **prime contractor** to the end customer
- Foreign vendor is the **subcontractor** (下請け) or component supplier
- SIer controls the customer relationship
- End customer may not know (or care) about the component supplier
- SIer assumes integration risk and delivery responsibility

### SIer Partnership Strategy
1. **Start small.** One project, one team, prove reliability.
2. **Train their engineers.** Japanese-language technical training is essential.
3. **Provide Japanese documentation.** API docs, implementation guides, troubleshooting.
4. **Support their sales.** Joint customer visits, proposal support, technical validation.
5. **Never go around them.** Direct customer contact without SIer permission is a fatal breach.
6. **Be patient.** SIer trust is built project by project over years.

### Multi-Tier SIer Structure
Typical Japanese prime contract structure:
```
End Customer (発注者)
  └── Prime SIer (元請け) — Owns relationship
        ├── Secondary SIer (二次請け) — Specialized capability
        │     └── Tertiary SIer (三次請け) — Further specialization
        └── Foreign Vendor (外資ベンダー) — Component/product
```

Each tier adds margin and communication layers. The foreign vendor at the bottom has least visibility but may be most essential to delivery.

## 4. OEM Partnerships

### Japanese OEM Model
- Japanese partner rebrands foreign product as their own
- Japanese partner handles: sales, support, documentation, localization
- Foreign vendor handles: product development, core updates
- Revenue: royalty or per-unit fee (not revenue share — Japanese partners resist disclosing end revenue)

### When OEM Makes Sense
- Partner has strong brand and existing customer base
- Product requires deep localization
- Vendor lacks Japanese market presence/capability
- Partner willing to invest in localization and support infrastructure

### OEM Risks
- Loss of brand visibility and market recognition
- Dependency on partner's sales performance
- Product feedback filtered through partner
- Difficult to unwind if partner performance disappoints
- IP protection concerns (source code access, reverse engineering)

## 5. Partner Selection & Due Diligence

### Red Flags in Japanese Partner Evaluation
- Promises "many customer relationships" without specifics
- Cannot name existing foreign vendor partnerships
- Unwilling to provide customer references
- Proposes equity JV before any commercial partnership
- High-pressure exclusivity demands early in discussions
- English-proficient negotiator but no Japanese technical staff
- Family-run business with unclear succession

### Due Diligence Checklist
- [ ] 登記簿謄本 (Corporate Registry) — verify incorporation, directors, capitalization
- [ ] 決算書 (Financial Statements) — past 3 years; verify through credit agency (帝国データバンク or 東京商工リサーチ)
- [ ] Existing partnerships with foreign vendors — interview references
- [ ] Engineer headcount and certifications
- [ ] Customer list in target vertical
- [ ] Support infrastructure (call center, ticketing, on-site capability)
- [ ] Visit their office — Japanese offices reveal organizational reality

## 6. Contract Structures

### Japanese Partner Contract Essentials
1. **基本契約書** (Basic Agreement) — Master terms; project-level agreements as addenda
2. **覚書 (Memorandum)** — Used for specific commercial terms; legally binding in Japan
3. **秘密保持契約 (NDA)** — Reciprocal; Japan recognizes both unilateral and mutual
4. **品質保証条項** — Quality assurance; defect response times; penalty structure
5. **管轄裁判所** (Jurisdiction) — Partners will push for Japanese court (Tokyo District Court is standard compromise)

### Unique Japanese Contract Features
- **協議条項** (Consultation Clause): "In case of dispute, parties shall consult in good faith" — legally significant in Japan; courts respect attempts at consultation
- **解除権** (Termination Rights): Notice periods are longer (6-12 months typical)
- **表明保証** (Representations & Warranties): Less extensive than US/UK norms
- **損害賠償制限** (Limitation of Liability): Cap is expected; unlimited liability is unusual
- **誠実義務** (Good Faith Duty): Implied in Japanese commercial contracts; broader than Western concept

## 7. Relationship Management

### The Partner Manager Role
Dedicated partner management is essential — not a side responsibility:
- Japanese-speaking (native or N1 equivalent)
- Based in Japan (not remote from overseas)
- Visits partner office monthly minimum
- Attends partner's customer meetings when invited
- Resolves issues personally, not via email

### Joint Business Planning
Annual joint planning cycle:
- **March-April:** Review past FY results
- **May-July:** Joint business plan for new FY
- **October:** Mid-year review and forecast adjustment
- **December:** Next year preliminary discussion

### Partner Enablement
- Japanese-language product training (quarterly)
- Certification program (Japanese-language exam)
- Sales playbook in Japanese
- Competitive battle cards in Japanese
- Joint reference customer development
- Co-marketing fund (共同マーケティング費用) — 1-3% of partner revenue

## 8. Industry-Specific Partnerships

### Hospital Group Partnerships
- University hospitals (大学病院) — Research collaboration gateway
- National hospital organizations (国立病院機構) — Centralized procurement
- Private hospital chains (医療法人) — Multiple decision-makers per institution
- Clinical research organizations (CRO) as partners
- Medical device distributors (医療機器販売業) — Licensed; regulatory pathway

### University Research Partnerships
- 共同研究 (Joint Research) — Contractual; IP terms negotiable
- 受託研究 (Contracted Research) — University executes; funder gets results
- 寄附講座 (Endowed Chair) — Long-term relationship building; expensive but effective
- Technology licensing from universities requires TLO (Technology Licensing Organization) engagement
