## 1. Keigo Levels & When to Use Each

Japanese politeness operates on three axes of keigo (敬語):

| Type | Japanese | Function | Usage |
|------|----------|----------|-------|
| Polite | 丁寧語 (Teineigo) | です・ます form | Default for all business; safe |
| Respectful | 尊敬語 (Sonkeigo) | Elevates the other party | Clients, senior partners, government officials |
| Humble | 謙譲語 (Kenjōgo) | Lowers yourself/your company | Describing your own actions to outsiders |

### Quick Keigo Selection Matrix

| Situation | To Customer | To Partner | Internal (within company) |
|-----------|------------|------------|---------------------------|
| Written proposal | 尊敬語 + 謙譲語 | 丁寧語 + light 尊敬語 | です・ます (or plain form) |
| Email | 尊敬語 + 謙譲語 | 丁寧語 + light 尊敬語 | Per company culture |
| Phone | 尊敬語 + 謙譲語 | 丁寧語 + light 尊敬語 | Casual polite |
| Meeting (you host) | 謙譲語 for company | 丁寧語 | N/A (Japanese speakers handle) |
| Meeting (you attend) | 尊敬語 for host | 丁寧語 | N/A |

### Common Keigo Conversions

| Plain | 尊敬語 (for them) | 謙譲語 (for us) |
|-------|-------------------|-----------------|
| 言う (say) | おっしゃる | 申す |
| する (do) | なさる | いたす |
| 行く (go) | いらっしゃる | 参る / 伺う |
| 来る (come) | いらっしゃる / お越しになる | 参る |
| 見る (see) | ご覧になる | 拝見する |
| 知っている (know) | ご存知 | 存じ上げる |
| 聞く (hear/ask) | お聞きになる | 伺う |
| 会社 (our company) | — | 弊社 (heisha) |
| 会社 (their company) | 御社 (onsha) | — |

**Key rule:** Never use 尊敬語 about yourself or your company. Never use 謙譲語 about the customer.

## 2. Japanese Business Email Format (ビジネスメール)

### Standard Structure
```
件名：【分類】用件の要点を簡潔に（20文字程度）

[Recipient Company] [Department]
[Name] [Title] 様

いつもお世話になっております。
[Your Company/Department/Name]でございます。

[Main body — concise, one topic per email]

[Closing — standard phrase]

[Your Name]
[Your Company]
[Department | Title]
〒[Postal Code] [Address]
TEL: [Phone]
MAIL: [Email]
URL: [Website]
```

### Subject Line (件名) Standards
| Classification | Prefix | Example |
|----------------|--------|---------|
| Request | 【お願い】 | 【お願い】来月の定例会議日程について |
| Notice | 【ご連絡】 | 【ご連絡】新製品リリースのご案内 |
| Confirmation | 【ご確認】 | 【ご確認】契約書案のご送付 |
| Thank You | 【御礼】 | 【御礼】先日のご訪問のお礼 |
| Urgent | 【緊急】 | 【緊急】システム障害発生のご報告 |
| Apology | 【お詫び】 | 【お詫び】納期遅延について |

### Opening & Closing Phrases

**Standard opening:**
- いつもお世話になっております。 (Always indebted for your support — standard)
- 平素より格別のご高配を賜り、厚く御礼申し上げます。(Very formal — for VIPs/government)

**Standard closing:**
- 引き続きよろしくお願いいたします。
- ご不明な点がございましたら、お気軽にご連絡くださいませ。

**Formal closing (proposals, government):**
- 何卒よろしくお願い申し上げます。

### Common Email Mistakes by Non-Native Speakers
- Using 様 (sama) for own company people → use 殿 (dono) for colleagues in formal internal docs
- ご苦労様です (gokurōsama) to customers → patronizing; use お疲れ様です to peers/subordinates only
- Overly casual closings (では、また) → never for business
- CC visible to all — Japanese recipients expect BCC for mass communications
- Attachments without mention in body → always list attachments (添付: ○○)

## 3. Meeting Protocol

### Before the Meeting
1. **Agenda in advance** (事前資料送付) — Send materials 3-5 business days before
2. **Participant list** (出席者一覧) — Both sides exchange names/titles beforehand
3. **Confirm seating** (席順) — Most senior furthest from door; host side opposite door
4. **Prepare Japanese materials** — Even if meeting is in English, have Japanese summary
5. **Arrive 10 minutes early** — Exactly on time = late in Japanese business

### During the Meeting
1. **Enter in hierarchy order** — Most senior enters last (Japanese side); visitor side follows their norms
2. **Start with introduction** — All participants introduced by role, not just name
3. **Meeting flow controlled by host** — Even in sales meetings, the customer "hosts"
4. **Don't interrupt silences** — Thinking silences are normal; don't fill them
5. **No phones/laptops open** — Unless presenting; conveys disinterest
6. **Senior person opens and closes** — Not the operational lead

### After the Meeting
- **お礼メール** (Thank-you email) — Same day or next morning
- **議事録** (Minutes) — Send within 2 business days
- Include: decisions made, action items with owners, next meeting proposal
- Send in Japanese (or bilingual if mixed attendees)

## 4. Business Card Exchange (名刺交換)

### The Ritual — Exact Sequence
1. Stand and face the other person
2. Present your card with both hands, Japanese side facing recipient
3. State: 「[Company]の[Name]でございます。よろしくお願いいたします。」
4. Receive their card with both hands, read it carefully (don't pocket immediately)
5. Comment on their title or company (「[Title]でいらっしゃいますか」)
6. Place card on the table in front of you (in seating order for group exchanges)
7. Never write on, fold, or play with business cards
8. At end of meeting, collect cards carefully into card holder (never back pocket)

### Your Business Card Requirements
- Double-sided: Japanese on one side, English on the other
- Title in Japanese must match organizational reality
- Include: company logo, name (furigana helpful), title, department, address, phone, email
- Quality paper stock — thin/flimsy cards signal low commitment
- Bring more than you think you need (30+ for a day of meetings)

## 5. Hierarchy Awareness

### Title Sensitivity
Japanese business titles are precise and significant:
- 部長 is not "manager" — it's a senior department head
- 課長 is not "team lead" — it's a formal management position with legal responsibilities
- Never inflate your title to match — Japanese counterparts verify
- Title mismatch (e.g., sending junior person to meet 部長) is disrespectful

### Communication by Level
| Your Level | Their Level | Appropriate Interaction |
|------------|-------------|------------------------|
| CEO/社長 | CEO/社長 | Strategic discussion; introductory courtesy; major deal sign-off |
| 部長 | 部長 | Commercial negotiation; relationship management meetings |
| 課長 | 課長 | Operational details; technical evaluation; day-to-day |
| 担当者 | 担当者 | Information gathering; technical Q&A; meeting coordination |

**Rule:** Don't go above or below your counterpart by more than one level without specific cause.

## 6. Japanese vs English — Language Strategy

### When Japanese is Required
- Contracts and legal documents (Japanese version governs)
- Government procurement proposals
- Customer support (end users)
- Official correspondence with traditional companies
- Any document requiring hanko/seal
- Press releases for Japanese media

### When English is Acceptable
- Technical documentation (with Japanese summary)
- Internal team communication (mixed-nationality team)
- API documentation (English is standard)
- International conference presentations

### When Bilingual is Optimal
- Product brochures and datasheets
- Website (Japanese primary, English secondary)
- Training materials (Japanese instruction, English technical terms)
- Meeting minutes (bilingual for mixed attendees)

### Strategic Language Choice
- **Speaking some Japanese builds trust** even if meeting switches to English
- **Err on the side of Japanese** for formal communication
- **Apologize for Japanese mistakes** — effort appreciated more than perfection
- **Don't assume English fluency** from English-language education background
- **Japanese business English** has its own conventions (e.g., "We would like to consider positively" = maybe)

## 7. Translation & Interpretation Strategy

### When to Use Professional Interpreters
- Contract negotiations
- High-stakes meetings with senior executives
- Technical discussions with nuanced terminology
- Government meetings (expected protocol)
- Any meeting where misunderstanding has high cost

### When Bilingual Staff Suffice
- Regular operational calls
- Informal relationship-building meetings
- Technical training (if engineer speaks both languages)
- Written translation of routine documents

### Interpretation Best Practices
- Brief the interpreter beforehand on context and terminology
- Speak in short segments (2-3 sentences max)
- Pause for interpretation — don't rush
- Look at your counterpart, not the interpreter
- Avoid jokes, idioms, sports metaphors (they don't translate)
- Provide written materials in both languages
- Budget interpretation time: meeting takes 2x normal duration

### Written Translation Priority
| Document | Priority | Translation Type |
|----------|----------|-------------------|
| Contract | Must | Professional legal translator |
| Proposal | Must | Professional + internal review |
| Product brief | High | Professional marketing translator |
| Technical docs | High | Bilingual engineer review |
| Internal comms | Medium | Bilingual staff |
| Social media | Medium | Native copywriter |

## 8. Escalation Language Pattern

### The Japanese Escalation Ladder (for written communication)
1. **Standard business communication** — 丁寧語, no legal references
2. **Concern expression** — "懸念しております" (we are concerned); suggests dissatisfaction
3. **Request for rectification** — "ご対応をお願いいたします" with specific deadline
4. **Formal demand** — 内容証明郵便 (Content-Certified Mail); time-stamped, legally significant
5. **Lawyer representation** — Attorney letterhead; signals litigation posture
6. **Pre-litigation notice** — "法的措置を検討せざるを得ません" (compelled to consider legal measures)
7. **Litigation** — Court filing

**Key escalation principle:** Japanese counterparties treat Stage 4 (内容証明) as a serious escalation. Do not deploy casually. Stage 6 language (法的措置) is never used lightly. Each step is meaningful and difficult to retract from.
