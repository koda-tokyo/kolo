|----------|----------|--------|-------------------|
| METI | 経済産業省 | Economy, Trade, Industry | Innovation-forward; pilot-friendly |
| MHLW | 厚生労働省 | Health, Labour, Welfare | Conservative; evidence-heavy; PMDA-aligned |

### Ministry Procurement Budget Cycles
- Japanese government fiscal year: April 1 – March 31
- Budget planning: August-December (previous year)
- Initial budgets approved: March (Diet vote)
- Supplementary budgets: typically November-December
- Best time to engage: June-August (after initial spending, before next year's planning)
- Year-end spending rush: February-March (remaining budgets must be used)

## 2. Public Tender System (入札制度)

### Tender Types

| Type | Japanese | When Used | Competitiveness |
|------|----------|-----------|-----------------|
| Open Tender | 一般競争入札 | Standard procurement > ¥1.6M | High; anyone can bid |
| Designated Tender | 指名競争入札 | Qualified vendors only | Moderate; by invitation |
| Discretionary Contract | 随意契約 | Urgent, proprietary, or small scale | Low; sole-source possible |

### Tender Process Flow
1. **Public Notice** (公示) — Published on ministry website and 官報 (Official Gazette)
2. **Bidder Qualification** (入札参加資格審査) — Annual qualification filing required
3. **Bid Submission** (入札書提出) — Sealed bids; strict deadline with no exceptions
4. **Bid Opening** (開札) — Public opening at specified date/time
5. **Evaluation** (審査) — Technical evaluation + price evaluation
6. **Award Decision** (落札決定) — Published; losing bidders can request debriefing
7. **Contract** (契約) — Formal contract executed within 30 days

### Qualification Requirements (入札参加資格)
- Annual filing with each ministry (separate per ministry)
- Financial statements (past 3 years)
- Tax payment certificates (納税証明書)
- Corporate registry extract (登記簿謄本) — within 3 months
- No criminal record for corporate officers
- Japanese corporate entity or registered branch (外国会社登記)

### Bid Evaluation Criteria
Japanese public tenders increasingly use 総合評価落札方式 (comprehensive evaluation):
- Technical score: 60-70% (capability, experience, methodology, personnel)
- Price score: 30-40% (lower price = higher score)
- Local presence, SME status, women-owned business can add points
- Low bid doesn't guarantee win — technical quality can outweigh price

## 3. Proposal Requirements

### Standard Proposal Structure (提案書)
1. **Cover Letter** (提案書提出届) — Formal submission letter with hanko
2. **Company Profile** (会社概要) — Registration details, financial standing, track record
3. **Implementation Plan** (実施計画) — Methodology, timeline, organization chart
4. **Personnel CVs** (担当者の経歴) — Key personnel resumes in Japanese format
5. **Similar Project Experience** (類似業務実績) — Past contracts with government/near-government
6. **Price Breakdown** (見積内訳書) — Detailed cost breakdown by category
7. **Compliance Certifications** — ISO, information security, etc.

### Critical Success Factors
- **Government track record matters more than commercial.** Ministries heavily weight prior government work.
- **Japanese-language proposals only.** English proposals are not evaluated (even if English is permitted).
- **Format compliance is non-negotiable.** Deviation from required format = disqualification.
- **Local presence.** Having a Japanese office and Japanese-speaking team is heavily weighted.
- **Subcontracting to Japanese firms** (再委託) is accepted and often expected for foreign primes.
- **Past performance with this specific ministry** is the strongest credential.

## 4. Compliance & Documentation

### Required Documentation Before Contract
- 誓約書 (Written Pledge) — Anti-Yakuza, anti-corruption
- 情報セキュリティ実施体制図 (Information Security Implementation Plan)
- 個人情報取扱特記事項同意書 (Personal Information Handling Agreement)
- 法令遵守体制図 (Legal Compliance Structure)
- Labor law compliance declaration (労働関係法令遵守)

### Contract Types
| Type | Japanese | Characteristics |
|------|----------|-----------------|
| Fixed Price | 請負契約 | Standard; deliverables specified |
| Quasi-Mandate | 準委任契約 | Effort-based; less deliverable-defined |
| Multi-Year | 複数年契約 | Up to 5 years for certain services |
| Framework | 包括契約 | Multiple task orders under master agreement |

### Intellectual Property
Default rule (without special agreement): Government owns IP created under contract.
- Negotiate IP retention clauses early in proposal
- Open source components must be explicitly disclosed
- 著作権法 (Copyright Act) Article 15 governs works made under contract

## 5. Ministry Relationship Building

### Key Principles
- Civil servant rotation (人事異動) happens every 1-3 years — build institutional, not personal
- Decision-makers are career bureaucrats, not political appointees
- Informal consultation (事前相談) before formal proposal is standard practice
- Advisory committees (審議会) influence policy — know who sits on them
- Alumni networks (OB/OG) matter — retired officials (天下り) provide access

### Engagement Channels
1. **Public comment periods** (パブリックコメント) — Visibility; demonstrate expertise
2. **Study groups** (研究会) — Ministry-organized; good for relationship building
3. **Industry associations** (業界団体) — Collective advocacy; ministry interlocutors
4. **Trade missions** (ミッション) — Through embassy/JETRO; for foreign companies
5. **Private briefings** (個別説明) — Once relationship established; present capabilities

### People to Know
- 課長補佐 (Kachō Hosa, Deputy Director) — Operational decision-maker; key relationship
- 係長 (Kakarichō, Section Chief) — Drafts documents; knows details
- 審議官 (Shingikan, Councillor) — Strategic/policy influence
- 事務次官 (Jimu Jikan, Administrative Vice Minister) — Career bureaucratic apex

## 6. Government PoC to Contract Pathway

### From Pilot to Procurement
1. **Research/Study Phase** (調査研究) — Commissioned study; easier entry point
2. **Pilot/PoC** (実証実験) — Often funded by ministry; prove local applicability
3. **Results Publication** (成果報告) — Published publicly; becomes referenced in next tender
4. **Budget Request** (概算要求) — Ministry includes solution in next FY budget
5. **Tender** (入札) — Often designed around PoC results (if PoC successful)
6. **Contract** (本契約) — Transition from PoC vendor to contract vendor

### The "Built for Us" Expectation
Japanese ministries expect solutions adapted to their specific procedures, not "off the shelf." Budget for:
- Japanese-language customization
- Integration with ministry legacy systems
- Documentation at Japanese government standard
- Training (in Japanese)
- On-site presence during transition

## 7. Subsidy Programs (補助金・助成金)

### Key Programs for Foreign Tech Companies
| Program | Administrator | Purpose | Typical Amount |
|---------|--------------|---------|----------------|
| J-Startup | METI | Startup ecosystem support | Non-financial (branding) |
| IT導入補助金 | METI/SMEA | SME digitalization | Up to ¥4.5M per SME |
| 研究開発税制 | METI | R&D tax credits | Tax credit up to 25% of R&D |
| 戦略的基盤技術高度化支援事業 (Sapoin) | METI | Advanced tech R&D | Up to ¥45M/year |
| AMED Grants | AMED/MHLW | Medical R&D | Variable; project-dependent |
| NEDO Projects | NEDO/METI | Energy/Environment tech | Large-scale R&D |

### Subsidy Application Strategy
- Partner with Japanese entity as joint applicant (many programs require this)
- Engage 中小企業診断士 (SME Consultant) for application writing
- Application complexity is high — budget 2-3 months for preparation
- Subsidy consultants (補助金コンサルタント) take 10-20% success fee
- Applications in Japanese only; kanji-heavy forms
