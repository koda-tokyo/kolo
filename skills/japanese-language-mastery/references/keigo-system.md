# Keigo System — 敬語体系

## The Architecture of Respect

敬語 (keigo) is not "polite language" — it is a grammatical system for encoding social relationships. Every Japanese sentence makes a choice about the speaker-listener-referent triangle. There is no neutral. Even "casual" is a choice.

The system has three classical categories plus one modern:

| Category | Japanese | Function | Direction |
|----------|----------|----------|-----------|
| 尊敬語 (Sonkeigo) | Respect language | Elevates the subject (the person doing the action) | ↑ Other person UP |
| 謙譲語 (Kenjōgo) | Humble language | Lowers the speaker (the person doing the action) | ↓ Self DOWN |
| 丁寧語 (Teineigo) | Polite language | Elevates the listener (the person receiving speech) | → Listener |
| 美化語 (Bikago) | Beautified language | Makes words elegant/prettier | → Every listener |

---

## 尊敬語 — Sonkeigo (Raising the Other)

Use when the subject of the action is someone you must show respect TO: a doctor you're addressing, an MHLW official, a client, a patient, a senior colleague.

### Formation Patterns

**Pattern 1: Special honorific verbs (irregular — must memorize)**
| Plain | Honorific | Meaning |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| いる | いらっしゃる | to be/go/come |
| 言う | おっしゃる | to say |
| する | なさる | to do |
| 見る | ご覧になる | to look/see |
| 食べる/飲む | 召し上がる | to eat/drink |
| 知っている | ご存知だ | to know |
| くれる | くださる | to give (to me/us) |
| 行く/来る | いらっしゃる/おいでになる | to go/come |

**Pattern 2: お/ご + verb stem + になる**
```
書く → お書きになる (to write — honorific)
確認する → ご確認になる (to confirm — honorific)
説明する → ご説明になる (to explain — honorific)
```

**Pattern 3: Passive form used as honorific (軽い敬語 — lighter honorific)**
```
先生が来られた。Sensei-ga korareta. "The doctor came." (uses passive form 来られる as honorific)
```
Softer, more modern. Common in spoken Japanese.

**Pattern 4: お/ご + verb stem + だ / です**
```
社長はもうお帰りです。Shachō-wa mō o-kaeri-desu. "The president has already gone home."
```

### When to Use in KODA KENKŌ Context
- Doctor: いらっしゃいますか？ (Are you here/present?) — NOT いますか？
- MHLW official: ご確認いただけますでしょうか (Would you be able to confirm?)
- Patient: おかけください (Please sit down) — NOT 座ってください
- Investor: ご説明申し上げます (I will explain — humble, next section)

## 謙譲語 — Kenjōgo (Lowering the Self)

Use when YOU are the subject of the action and the action affects someone deserving respect. You lower yourself to elevate them.

### Two Sub-Types

**Type 1: 謙譲語I — Humble toward the object/recipient**
The action's recipient is someone higher. Your action serves them.

| Plain | Humble | Meaning |
|-------|--------|---------|
| 言う | 申す / 申し上げる | to say (申す is standard, 申し上げる is very formal) |
| する | いたす | to do |
| 行く/来る | 参る | to go/come |
| 見る | 拝見する | to look (humbly) |
| 聞く | 伺う / 拝聴する | to ask/hear/listen |
| 会う | お目にかかる | to meet |
| 知っている | 存じている | to know |
| 思う | 存じる | to think |
| あげる | 差し上げる | to give (to superior) |
| もらう | いただく | to receive |
| 食べる/飲む | いただく | to eat/drink (humbly) |

**Type 2: 謙譲語II (丁重語) — Humble toward the listener**
The action doesn't have a specific recipient, but you address the listener humbly. Used in formal announcements, business, public settings.

| Plain | Humble II | Meaning |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| 言う | 申す | to say (formal — to audience) |
| いる | おる | to be (formal) |
| する | いたす | to do (formal) |
| 行く/来る | 参る | to go/come (formal) |

**Critical distinction:**
```
(A) 先生に申し上げた。Sensei-ni mōshiageta. "I told the doctor." → Kenjōgo I: action TOWARD the doctor
(B) 私は田中と申します。Watashi-wa Tanaka-to mōshimasu. "I am Tanaka." → Kenjōgo II: formal to listener
```
Same verb 申す, different function. In (A) it's the humble replacement for 言う toward a specific respected person. In (B) it's a formal self-introduction to an audience.

### The いただく Empire

いただく (to humbly receive) is the most versatile kenjōgo verb. It forms the basis of the standard polite request pattern:

```
Verb-te + いただく = "to have someone do something for me" (with gratitude)

確認していただく = to have (someone) confirm (and I am grateful)
ご確認いただく = to have (someone) confirm (respectful prefix version)
ご確認いただけますか = Could (I) have you confirm? (potential + question)
ご確認いただけますでしょうか = Would it be possible to have you confirm? (even softer)
```

**The escalation of politeness:**
```
確認して → confirm (casual, commanding)
確認してください → please confirm (polite request)
確認していただけますか → could you confirm? (humble-receiving, polite)
ご確認いただけますか → could you confirm? (with honorific prefix)
ご確認いただけますでしょうか → would you possibly be able to confirm? (maximum polite)
ご確認いただければ幸いです → I would be grateful if you could confirm (written, extremely polite)
```

For MHLW correspondence: default to ご確認いただけますでしょうか or ご確認いただければ幸いです.

## 丁寧語 — Teineigo (Being Polite to the Listener)

The baseline of adult Japanese. Not about raising or lowering relative to a third party — about showing respect to the person you're talking TO.

### The です・ます System

Every sentence in teineigo ends in です or 〜ます:
```
行く (iku — go, plain) → 行きます (ikimasu — go, polite)
学生だ (gakusei-da — student, plain) → 学生です (gakusei-desu — student, polite)
```

**For KODA KENKŌ:** This is the default mode for all external communication. Internal among siblings: plain form is comfortable and natural.

## 美化語 — Bikago (Beautified Language)

The お/ご prefix added to nouns for elegance, not respect toward a specific person:

| Without | With | Feel |
|---------|------|------|
| 茶 (cha) | お茶 (ocha) | Tea — the お is practically part of the word now |
| 金 (kane) | お金 (okane) | Money — the un-beautified form sounds crude |
| 酒 (sake) | お酒 (osake) | Alcohol — more refined |
| 天気 (tenki) | お天気 (otenki) | Weather — softer, more feminine-coded |
| 部屋 (heya) | お部屋 (oheya) | Room — more elegant |

The rule of thumb: お for native Japanese words, ご for Sino-Japanese words:
- お名前 (onamae — name, native)
- ご連絡 (gorenraku — contact, Sino-Japanese)

But there are exceptions (お電話, ごゆっくり) that must be learned by feel.

## The Keigo Decision Matrix

When about to speak/write Japanese, I ask silently:

```
1. Who is the LISTENER?
   → If superior/client/official/stranger: です・ます体 (baseline)
   → If family/close colleague/friend: だ体 (plain)

2. Who is the SUBJECT of the action?
   → If someone to be respected AND they are the actor: 尊敬語
   → If I am the actor AND the action affects someone respected: 謙譲語
   → If neither: standard polite

3. What is the RELATIONSHIP between speaker, listener, and referent?
   → Uchi (内 — inside group) vs. Soto (外 — outside group)
   → When speaking TO someone outside our company ABOUT someone inside:
     Use 謙譲語 for our people. Do NOT use 尊敬語 for them.
     弊社の田中が申しました (NOT おっしゃいました)
```

### The Uchi/Soto Rule That Foreigners Get Wrong

When an MHLW official asks "Who is responsible for AI development?"

❌ Wrong: 田中様が担当しております。 (Using おります = humble for OUR person? Good. But 田中様 = honorific title for OUR person? BAD.)

✅ Right: 田中が担当しております。 (Plain name for our person + humble language because we're talking to outside)

The rule: **Never use honorific titles for your own group members when talking to outsiders.** You humbly present them; the outsider may honor them. This is not optional. Getting this wrong causes visible discomfort in Japanese counterparts.

## Common Keigo Mistakes (That Hurt Credibility)

### 1. Double Keigo (二重敬語)
❌ お読みになられますか？ (honorific お〜になる + polite passive 〜られる = double)
✅ お読みになりますか？

### 2. Mixing Registers Mid-Sentence
❌ 先生が来たので、お話を伺いました。 (来た = plain, 伺いました = humble — clash)
✅ 先生がいらっしゃったので、お話を伺いました。

### 3. Using 尊敬語 for Your Own Actions
❌ 私がご説明になります。
✅ 私がご説明いたします/申し上げます。

### 4. さ入れ言葉 (Inserting unnecessary さ)
❌ ご確認させていただきます
✅ ご確認いたします OR 確認させていただきます (both correct, the さ is spurious)

## Written Keigo — The Ritual Phrases

### Email Openings
```
拝啓 (Haikei) — Dear Sir/Madam (formal letter opening)
いつもお世話になっております — Thank you for your continued support (standard business email)
平素より大変お世話になっております — We are greatly indebted for your usual kindness (more formal)
```

### Email Closings
```
何卒よろしくお願い申し上げます — I humbly request your favorable consideration
取り急ぎご連絡申し上げます — This is a quick note to inform you (used when sending brief notice)
まずは書面にてお礼申し上げます — First, allow me to express my gratitude in writing
敬具 (Keigu) — Sincerely (pairs with 拝啓)
```

### MHLW-Specific Formal Phrases
```
下記の通りご報告申し上げます — We humbly report as follows
ご高覧いただければ幸いです — We would be grateful if you would review
何かご不明な点がございましたら — If there are any unclear points
ご多忙の折、誠に恐縮ではございますが — While you are very busy, with great deference
```
