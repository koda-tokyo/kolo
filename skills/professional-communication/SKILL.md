# Professional Communication — Board Papers, Investor Updates, Proposals & Japanese Business Writing

## Purpose
Consistent, high-quality professional communication across all formats: board papers, investor updates, client proposals, pitch decks, Japanese business correspondence (ビジネス文書), internal memos, and executive summaries. Not templates — frameworks for clarity, persuasion, and professionalism that adapt to context while maintaining quality. Built for KodaSōken, KODA KENKŌ, and KoLo external communications.

## Triggers
Use this skill for:
- Writing board papers, board decks, or director communications
- Investor updates, fundraising materials, pitch narratives
- Client proposals, SOWs (Scope of Work), project charters
- Japanese business email, 始末書, 稟議書, 報告書
- Executive summaries of complex technical work for non-technical audiences
- Press releases, public announcements, product communications
- Internal memos that need to be crisp, clear, and actionable
- Any writing task where the audience is important, busy, and judging you

## Core Knowledge

### The First Principle of Professional Writing

> **The reader's time is more valuable than yours.**

If your memo takes 15 minutes to read and could have been 5, you've stolen 10 minutes from every reader. Multiply by the number of readers. That's the cost of poor writing.

### The 3-Question Test (Before You Write)

1. **Who is reading this?** (role, knowledge level, what they care about)
2. **What do I want them to DO after reading?** (one action, not five)
3. **What do they NEED to know to take that action?** (cut everything else)

If you can't answer all three in one sentence each, you're not ready to write.

### Executive Summary Framework

For every document longer than 2 pages, lead with:

```
Paragraph 1: WHAT — The one-sentence situation. "We are at X stage with Y."
Paragraph 2: SO WHAT — Why it matters. The stakes.
Paragraph 3: NOW WHAT — The decision needed, the question, the next step.
```

**The executive summary must stand alone.** If a board member reads only this and nothing else, they should know exactly what's happening and what's expected of them.

### Board Paper Structure

```markdown
# [Topic] — Board Paper

**Date:** [YYYY-MM-DD]
**Author:** [Name / Role]
**Decision Required:** [Approve | Note | Discuss | Endorse]

## Executive Summary (≤200 words)
[WHAT / SO WHAT / NOW WHAT]

## Situation (≤500 words)
- Background (2-3 sentences — what got us here)
- Current state (facts, numbers, timeline)
- Key stakeholders and their positions

## Analysis (≤750 words)
- Options considered (2-4 alternatives with pros/cons)
- Recommendation and rationale
- Risks and mitigations
- Financial implications (if applicable)

## Next Steps
- [ ] Decision point: [exactly what the board is being asked to do]
- [ ] If approved: immediate next action, timeline, accountable person

## Appendices (if needed)
- Supporting data, detailed financials, reference documents
```

### Investor Update Structure

Monthly or quarterly investor communication:

```
Subject: [Company] Update — [Month/Quarter] [Year]

Hi [Name],

[One sentence on the most important thing that happened this period.]

## Highlights
- [Win 1 — number if possible]
- [Win 2]
- [Win 3]

## By the Numbers
- Revenue: ¥X (↑Y% MoM)
- Users/Customers: X (↑Y)
- Cash: ¥X (X months runway)
- Team: X people

## What We're Focused On
- [Priority 1 with 1-sentence why]
- [Priority 2 with 1-sentence why]

## Asks
- [Specific ask if any — intro, feedback, reference check]

Best,
[Name]
```

### Client Proposal Structure

```
1. Understanding (1 page)
   — "Here's what we heard you say." Prove you listened.

2. The Opportunity (1 page)
   — What's possible. Frame as transformation, not features.

3. Our Approach (2-3 pages)
   — How we'd work together. Phases, not features.
   — Team: who specifically, with relevant credentials

4. Evidence (1-2 pages)
   — Relevant case studies, past results, why us

5. Timeline & Investment (1 page)
   — Key milestones, deliverables, dates
   — Price and what's included (and what isn't)

6. Next Step (1 page)
   — One clear call to action: "We propose a [X-week] discovery phase starting [date] at [price]."

Total: 7-9 pages. Never more than 12.
```

### Japanese Business Writing (ビジネス文書)

#### ビジネスメール (Business Email)

**Structure:**
```
件名: 【[会社名/案件名]】[簡潔な内容]

[相手先企業名]
[部署名]
[氏名] 様

いつもお世話になっております。
[自社名]の[自分の名前]でございます。

[本文 — 簡潔に]
[用件を先に。説明は後。一文は短く。]

[次のアクション — 明確に]
[「◯日までにご返信いただけますと幸いです」など]

何卒よろしくお願い申し上げます。

---
[署名 — 社名、氏名、役職、連絡先]
```

**Key conventions:**
- 様 for external, 殿 is archaic/internal (almost never use)
- 拝啓...敬具 for formal letters (not email)
- CC: 皆様 instead of CC: さん (CC is public — use respectful neutral)
- Never use 了解しました to superiors — use 承知しました or かしこまりました
- Attachments: 「○○の資料を添付いたします」not 「添付しました」

#### 企画書 (Internal Proposal / Ringi-sho)

```
1. 表紙 — タイトル、作成日、作成者、提出先
2. 提案概要 (1枚) — WHAT, WHY, HOW MUCH
3. 背景・現状分析 — なぜ今必要なのか
4. 提案内容の詳細 — 具体的な計画
5. 期待される効果 — 定量的に、できれば金額で
6. 想定されるリスクと対策 — 正直に書く
7. 実施スケジュール — マイルストーン
8. 必要なリソース（人・予算） — 過小評価しない
9. まとめ — 決裁をお願いしたいポイントを再度明確に
```

### The 1-Page Memo (Amazon 6-Pager Adapted)

For when something is complex but the reader is busy:

```
Page 1: The press release
  — If this project succeeds, what's the headline?
  — Written in past tense, as if already done
  — Forces clarity: if you can't write the press release, you don't know what you're building

Pages 2-6 (if needed):
  — FAQ: the 5 hardest questions and honest answers
  — Supporting data
  — Alternatives considered
```

### Writing Quality Standards

#### Clarity over Cleverness
```
❌ "We leverage a multi-modal architecture to facilitate longitudinal patient outcomes optimization"
✅ "Our system helps doctors track whether patients get better over time"
```

#### Facts over Adjectives
```
❌ "Industry-leading, cutting-edge, revolutionary platform"
✅ "Processes 10,000 documents per day. Deployed in 3 hospitals. 94% accuracy in clinical audit."
```

#### Active over Passive
```
❌ "A decision was made to proceed with the deployment"
✅ "The team decided to deploy on August 12"
```

#### Specific over Vague
```
❌ "Significant improvement in performance"
✅ "Response time decreased from 3.2s to 0.8s (75% faster)"
```

### Tone Calibration

| Context | Tone | Formality | Example Opening |
|---------|------|-----------|-----------------|
| Board paper | Direct, understated confidence | High | "The Board is asked to approve..." |
| Investor update | Warm but data-driven | Medium | "Here's what happened this month." |
| Client proposal | Confident but not arrogant | Medium-High | "Based on our understanding of your needs..." |
| Internal memo | Clear, peer-to-peer | Low-Medium | "Here's where we are and what's next." |
| Japanese external email | Deferential, precise | High | "平素より大変お世話になっております。" |
| Japanese internal (同僚) | Warm, professional | Medium | "お疲れ様です。" |
| Press release | Factual, newsworthy | Medium-High | "[City, Date] — [Company] today announced..." |
| Crisis communication | Direct, taking responsibility | High | "We want to inform you of an incident..." |

### The 24-Hour Rule

For anything important going to an external audience: write it, then wait 24 hours. Re-read with fresh eyes. You will find problems you couldn't see while writing.

If you can't wait 24 hours, have someone else read it. If you can't do that, read it aloud to yourself. Writing that sounds good in your head often sounds wrong spoken.

### Document Quality Checklist

Before sending anything to an external reader:

- [ ] The reader knows within 10 seconds what this is about and what's expected of them
- [ ] Every paragraph earns its place (the "so what?" test)
- [ ] Numbers have context (is ¥10M a lot or a little in this context?)
- [ ] Acronyms spelled out on first use
- [ ] Japanese/English consistency (敬語 level correct, no mixture of です・ます and である)
- [ ] Names, titles, company names verified (triple-check — getting a name wrong is the fastest way to lose credibility)
- [ ] Attachments actually attached
- [ ] Links work
- [ ] Read aloud: does it sound like something a professional human would say?

### Anti-Patterns

- ❌ Starting with background instead of the point (burying the lede)
- ❌ Writing to impress rather than to communicate
- ❌ Using jargon the reader might not know (define or cut)
- ❌ Passive voice to avoid responsibility ("mistakes were made")
- ❌ Wall of text — if it's more than 4 lines without a break, add a heading or a line break
- ❌ Japanese: mixing 敬語 levels (尊敬語・謙譲語・丁寧語) incorrectly
- ❌ Japanese: using 殿 (との) for external recipients (use 様)
- ❌ Assuming the reader has read the background material (they haven't)
- ❌ More than 3 asks in one communication (the reader does 0)
